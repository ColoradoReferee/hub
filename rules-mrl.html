BEFORE THE MATCH
  • Introduce yourself to each coach and ask for the team roster. Verify every player and coach against the electronic roster or approved digital player pass.
  • No valid roster? The game still goes ahead. Get a written list of names and numbers from that coach, tell the other coach, and send CSA a detailed report. It is not grounds for a protest.
  • Note anyone sitting out a suspension; you confirm it in the game report. Inspect every player's equipment.
  • Each team brings one or more balls; you pick. Field fitness is your call and it is final; permit holders can still close a field.
  • One whistle only. The diagonal system is the only system; a two-whistle game can cost you your game fee.
9U AND 10U: THE BUILD-OUT LINE
The build-out line sits halfway between the penalty area and the halfway line.

When the goalkeeper has the ball in hand or a goal kick, the opponents retreat behind it until the ball is in play. From the keeper's hands, the ball is in play when it leaves them; from a goal kick, when it is kicked and clearly moves.

  • No punts and no drop-kicks. The keeper throws, rolls or passes with the feet. A punt or drop-kick: stop play, indirect free kick where the offense occurred (on the penalty area line if it happened inside).
  • Offside only between the build-out line and the goal line. Between the halfway line and the build-out line there is no offside.
  • No deliberate heading. Indirect free kick from the spot; inside the penalty area, move it to the nearest point outside and give the indirect free kick there.
SUBSTITUTIONS AND TIMING
  • Unlimited, at any stoppage, with your permission.
  • A subbed-off player has 10 seconds to leave. Too slow: the sub waits until the first stoppage after 1 minute.
  • Goalkeeper hands: 8 seconds max. Count down the last 5 visibly; over time is a corner kick to the opponents.
  • Throw-in delay: whistle, count 5 visibly; over time is a throw-in to the opponents.
  • Goal kick delay: whistle, count 5 visibly; still not taken is a corner kick to the opponents.
  • No overtime in league matches.
UNIFORMS AND EQUIPMENT
  • Home (listed first) wears dark when colors clash; visitors light. Keepers change as you direct.
  • No earrings (silicone flat-back protectors are fine) and no hard hair clips. Taping is not allowed. Accessories only if not dangerous and safely, securely covered.
  • Failure to notice illegal equipment is not grounds for a protest.
FIELD AND SIDELINE
  • Goals anchored. 9U and 10U goals 6 × 18 ft or 6.5 × 18.5 ft; 11U and 12U goals 6.5 × 18.5 ft or 7 × 21 ft; 13U to 19U goals 8 × 24 ft. Cones and flags are not proper markings; the home team is responsible for the field.
  • Both teams on one side, spectators on the other when feasible. The home coach picks sides; you enforce it.
  • Spectators at least 5 feet from touchlines and goal lines, or behind a spectator line. No one behind the goal lines unless seated in bleachers.
  • You may warn, caution or send off team officials for irresponsible behavior in the technical area. Disruptive spectator behavior from the touchline is misconduct: report it at caution or send-off level, and the Referee Abuse Prevention Policy applies.
AFTER THE MATCH
Score and game report in Assignr within 24 hours

Cautions, send-offs, serious injuries and completed sit-outs. Reports later than 48 hours, or missing key details, can cost you an administrative penalty, up to the game fee.

  • Confirm with your crew every caution, send-off and misconduct: who, which team, what offense.
  • Return team documents to the coaches.
  • Report unsporting or disruptive spectator behavior.
  • Game not played but you were there? File an incident report explaining why. Game not played for lack of a referee: the coaches report it, not you.
  • Keep your notes; you may be asked to explain an incident at a hearing.
